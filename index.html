Soil Responses to Mineral Nitrogen and Carbon-Bearing Amendments in Moso Bamboo Forests: A Meta-AnalysisCORE9
Authors: A. Qamar, Nasir Shad, Abbas Ali, Nizar Nawaz, Chun-Ce Guo
Venue: Plants
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Moso bamboo (Phyllostachys edulis) plantations in subtropical China are intensively fertilized, yet it remains unclear how soil responses differ between mineral nitrogen and carbon-bearing amendments, and how far they depend on the nitrogen application rate. This meta-analysis of 69 articles covered four fertilization regimes (N, nitrogen alone; NPK, balanced mineral fertilizer; N+OM, nitrogen with organic carbon; B, biochar alone). We found that soil pH and organic carbon (SOC) responded in opposite directions to nitrogen alone and to the carbon-bearing amendments. Nitrogen alone acidified the soil (−5.3%) and reduced SOC (−11.4%), whereas B and N+OM raised pH (+9.5%, +3.4%) and SOC (+14.0%, +28.7%); NPK also raised SOC (+17.9%) but slightly lowered pH (−1.7%). The mineral regimes increased nitrate (+72.9% under N) but not total nitrogen, which rose only under N+OM (+33.3%). Microbial biomass carbon (MBC) increased under N, NPK and N+OM (+9.8% to +20.6%), whereas enzyme activities were largely unchanged. Nitrogen alone increased cumulative nitrous oxide emission (+18.7%) and reduced methane uptake (−15.5%), whereas N+OM lowered the nitrous oxide emission rate (−5.9%) and increased methane uptake (+9.0%). Each additional 100 kg N ha−1 yr−1 raised MBC by 1.9% and ammonium nitrogen by 4.7% without altering SOC, although most of the application rates tested were below 100 kg N ha−1 yr−1. Within this range, adding an exogenous carbon source to nitrogen may limit soil acidification and SOC depletion.

Physiological responses and tolerance of Idesia polycarpa seedlings under simulated rhizosphere acidificationCORE6
Authors: Zu-Wei Hu, Wen-Wen Zhong, Ya-Nan Ma, Can Cao, Yu-Lan Bei...
Venue: Frontiers in Plant Science
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil acidification poses a growing threat to forest seedling establishment, yet the physiological responses and tolerance limits of many multipurpose woody species under increasing acidity remain poorly understood. Idesia polycarpa Maxim. is an emerging woody oil tree with high ecological and economic value, but its physiological responses to root-zone acidification are largely unknown. We subjected one-year-old I. polycarpa seedlings to 50 d of root-zone irrigation with acidified solutions at pH 5.5, 4.5, and 3.5, with deionized water as the control, and monitored visible injury, photosynthetic pigments, malondialdehyde (MDA), osmotic adjustment substances, and the activities of peroxidase (POD) and superoxide dismutase (SOD). Visible injury increased with both acidity and exposure duration, and the cumulative injury index reached 85.00% under pH 3.5, 40.00% under pH 4.5, and 16.67% under pH 5.5 after 50 d, while CK remained at 0%. Under pH 3.5, photosynthetic pigment contents showed pronounced temporal fluctuations, with marked reductions at 20 and 50 d and partial recovery during the intermediate stages. MDA content also exhibited a non-monotonic response, with significantly higher values under pH 3.5 at several middle and late sampling times. Soluble sugar, proline, and soluble protein showed stage- and treatment-dependent changes rather than continuous accumulation or decline. POD activity under pH 3.5 reached approximately 3.55-fold the CK value at 10 d and subsequently declined, whereas SOD activity fluctuated markedly among treatments and sampling times. Correlation analysis revealed coordinated variation among photosynthetic pigments, MDA, osmotic adjustment substances, and the two measured antioxidant enzymes, but these associations do not establish causal relationships. Overall, seedlings under pH 5.5 and pH 4.5 showed partial physiological adjustment, whereas pH 3.5 was associated with substantially greater visible injury and physiological disturbance. The contrasting responses between the pH 4.5 and pH 3.5 irrigation treatments suggest a potential physiological tolerance boundary within this tested acidity interval under the present controlled conditions, rather than a statistically defined biological breakpoint.

Authors: Peter Coaguila-Rodriguez, A. Cerna-Cueva
Venue: Agronomía Mesoamericana
Published: 2026-10-26

Abstract
Introduction. District-level soil assessments may obscure local constraints relevant to cacao soil management. Objective. To characterize and prioritize environmental edaphic constraints in cacao soils of the Peruvian Amazon, with emphasis on soil acidity, available phosphorus, aluminum saturation, and available cadmium at the locality scale. Materials and methods. A total of 1,842 surface soil samples (0–20 cm), collected between April and July 2021 in 84 localities in the districts of Puerto Bermúdez and Constitución, were analyzed. Physical and chemical soil variables were evaluated using descriptive statistics, district-level comparisons, locality-level summaries, co-occurrence analysis, bivariate relationships, principal component analysis, and exploratory clustering. Results. Median soil pH was 4.65, median available phosphorus was 5.27 mg kg⁻¹, and median aluminum saturation was 39.21% (1,539 valid determinations). The co-occurrence of pH below 5.5, available phosphorus below 7 mg kg⁻¹, and aluminum saturation of at least 30% was observed in 757 samples (41.1%), indicating the combined occurrence of acidity, low phosphorus, and high aluminum saturation. Available cadmium had a median of 0.035 mg kg⁻¹ but showed a heterogeneous distribution, with localized signals in 13 localities. Conclusions. The locality scale distinguished widespread chemical constraints from localized available-cadmium signals and provided criteria for prioritizing soil monitoring.

Towards an integrated ecosystem monitoring framework at the LTER Parangalitsa siteCORE6

Abstract
Introduction Long-term ecosystem research increasingly relies on the integration and harmonization of complementary environmental observations to improve our understanding of ecosystem functioning and responses to environmental change (Mollenhauer et al. 2018, Zacharias et al. 2026). Extended time series provide an essential temporal perspective for detecting ecosystem responses to environmental change and understanding interactions among multiple environmental drivers (Jones and Driscoll 2022). Long-term observations at the site provide information on climatic conditions and key components of forest ecosystem status and functioning. However, these datasets originate from different monitoring programs, periods and methodological frameworks, which limits their direct integration and the assessment of interactions among atmospheric, soil and vegetation processes. The harmonization of existing long-term records with newly established observations therefore represents an important step towards a more comprehensive ecosystem-level monitoring framework. The aim of this work is to present the ongoing development of an integrated monitoring framework at the LTER Parangalitsa site, combining historical and long-term datasets with newly introduced measurements of complementary ecosystem variables. The framework is intended to improve the capacity to assess ecosystem responses to environmental change and to support future integrated analyses of this old-growth mountain forest ecosystem. Materials and methods The LTER Parangalitsa site, located within the old-growth Norway spruce forests of the Parangalitsa Biosphere Reserve (Rila Mountains, Bulgaria), represents a long-term forest monitoring and research site within the ICP Forests, LTER-Bulgaria and eLTER networks. The monitoring programme combines historical and contemporary observations covering multiple ecosystem compartments. Meteorological records dating back to 1980 are complemented by long-term environmental monitoring conducted within the ICP Forests programme, in which the site has participated since 1990. Soil monitoring follows established ICP Forests methodological guidelines and includes periodic assessments of soil physical and chemical properties, such as soil organic carbon, total nitrogen, soil acidity, bulk density, texture and nutrient status. Together, these datasets provide a basis for investigating long-term ecosystem dynamics under natural, unmanaged forest conditions. The ongoing expansion of the monitoring programme is guided by the eLTER Standard Observations framework, aiming to progressively cover relevant ecosystem variables and improve methodological harmonization across monitoring components. As part of this development, Leaf Area Index (LAI) measurements were initiated in 2026 to characterize canopy structure and seasonal dynamics, while litterfall monitoring provides information on aboveground organic matter inputs. Continuous monitoring of shallow subsurface water has also been established using a multiparameter EXO1 sonde installed in a dedicated 2-m-deep monitoring well. The recorded parameters include temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, oxidation-reduction potential, salinity and total dissolved solids. Together, these complementary observations establish a foundation for linking climatic variability, canopy dynamics, soil processes and subsurface hydrological conditions within an integrated ecosystem monitoring framework. Results and discussion The compilation of historical and contemporary monitoring records reveals substantial differences in the temporal coverage and frequency of ecosystem observations at the LTER Parangalitsa site (Table 1). Meteorological records extend back to 1979, while soil observations have been available since the early 1990s. Periodic assessments of forest floor properties and aboveground biomass provide additional information on forest ecosystem characteristics. More recently, the monitoring programme has expanded to include automated measurements, molecular biodiversity observations and additional hydrological and vegetation-related variables. The compiled datasets cover multiple ecosystem compartments but differ considerably in temporal continuity, sampling frequency and methodological approaches. Historical observations provide a valuable long-term perspective, whereas recently introduced measurements extend the range of ecosystem variables being monitored. Their integration will require careful consideration of methodological compatibility and temporal resolution. The ongoing development of the monitoring programme, guided by the eLTER Standard Observations framework, provides a basis for improving data harmonization and supporting future integrated assessments of ecosystem dynamics. Conclusions The LTER Parangalitsa site provides a valuable basis for integrated ecosystem monitoring through the combination of historical records, established long-term observations and newly introduced measurements. The progressive alignment of these monitoring components with the eLTER Standard Observations framework is expected to improve data comparability and strengthen the capacity for cross-compartment analyses. This developing framework will support future assessments of long-term ecosystem dynamics and responses to environmental change in old-growth mountain forests.
Evidence for temporal decoupling of olivine weathering and inorganic carbon removal in unsaturated soil columnsPROXY4
Authors: Malgorzata Rizzi, Elliot Chang, C. Pallud
Venue: Frontiers in Climate
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW) is a nature-based carbon dioxide (CO 2 ) removal technology that speeds the natural weathering process of rocks by applying finely ground silicate rocks over the land surface. It has been proposed that ERW can remove between 0.5 and 2 Gt CO 2 year −1 when deployed at scale. To investigate how soil porewater responds to the dissolution of silicate dust during enhanced rock weathering, we conducted unsaturated soil column experiments amended with olivine (Mg 2 SiO 4 ). Since the main principle of ERW-based carbon removal is the reaction of atmospheric CO 2 dissolved in rainwater with silicate rocks, leading to release of base cations and bicarbonate ions, we measured cations, anions, pH, alkalinity, dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) and dissolved organic carbon (DOC) in the leached effluent. Complementary solid-phase soil measurements of exchangeable cations, pH, total inorganic carbon (TIC) and total organic carbon (TOC) were performed to improve understanding of weathering product transport and retention, while aqueous speciation calculations provided saturation state information of relevant secondary mineral phases. We demonstrate that measurable olivine weathering can occur without proportional short-term export of bicarbonate or DIC, and estimates of potential CO 2 removal based on weathering-derived cation release exceeded realized inorganic CO 2 removal quantified from bicarbonate and DIC export. Furthermore, the observed increase in DOC export and decrease in surface-soil organic carbon suggest that enhanced weathering may alter soil organic carbon dynamics. The discrepancy between weathering-derived cation release and inorganic carbon export is consistent with temporal decoupling between weathering and realized carbon removal, likely caused by retention of weathering-derived products within the soil. Although the mechanisms underlying the observed changes in organic carbon dynamics require further investigation, these results indicate that ERW may affect soil carbon pools beyond inorganic carbon alone. Although alkalinity export was lower than would be predicted from weathering rates alone, quantification of silicate dissolution remains an important upper-bound indicator of future carbon removal potential. These findings highlight the importance of considering soil retention processes, organic carbon dynamics, and transport timescales when interpreting ERW monitoring data and quantifying carbon removal in agricultural soils.

Abstract
ABSTRACT Near-natural silviculture practices have gained prominence as sustainable forestry strategies for optimizing stand site conditions and enhancing forest productivity. The performance of these ecosystem processes is closely related to soil microorganisms. However, there is still a lack of systematic characterization of how microorganisms respond to such silvicultural interventions, and this critical knowledge gap limits our ability to predict long-term forest ecosystem trajectories. Moreover, the specific functions of microbial communities in mixed plantations remain largely unknown. Here, we analyzed the compositional and functional profiles of bacterial and fungal communities across a gradient of stand types, including nine near-natural mixed Cunninghamia lanceolata and Phoebe bournei plantations (a 3 × 3 factorial design with three densities per species) and one pure C. lanceolata stand. The results showed that mixed planting enhanced bacterial and fungal diversity and altered their community structures by accelerating soil organic matter decomposition and total nitrogen mineralization, while increasing soil pH, available phosphorus, and available potassium. Notably, mixed planting increased the relative abundance of bacteria involved in carbon and nitrogen metabolism, along with saprophytic and symbiotic fungi, while reducing that of pathogenic fungi. Moreover, microbial community variation was stronger among mixed planting treatments than between soil depths, with reduced C. lanceolata density exerting a greater influence than increased P. bournei density. These findings underscore that tree diversity and planting density collectively determine soil microbial assembly. Consequently, this study provides empirical evidence to predict the impact of near-natural silviculture on microbial communities and offers insights for optimizing the management of C. lanceolata plantations. Authors: J. Ramírez-Gil, Julián David Gómez-Ballen, Angel Rebollar Alviter, J. C. Angel, Freddy Fernando Garcés-Obando
Venue: Phytopathology
Published: 2026-10-26

Abstract
The Polerovirus Sugarcane yellow leaf virus (SCYLV) causes one of the most economically important viral diseases of sugarcane worldwide, including Colombia, where sugarcane is a strategic crop and the virus has high phytosanitary relevance but limited epidemiological characterization. This study provides the first integrative quantitative and multimodel epidemiological assessment of SCYLV in the country, using a 22-year surveillance dataset (1999 to 2020) from Colombia's main sugarcane-producing region. The analysis integrated data science, classical statistics, machine learning, and spatial epidemiology to identify temporal trends, spatiotemporal aggregation, and environmental and agronomic drivers of disease risk. Data science procedures supported data curation, exploratory analysis, and detection of patterns in SCYLV presence-absence, incidence, and prevalence across the historical series. Risk factor identification used logistic regression modeling of presence-absence data to estimate adjusted associations, and the machine learning framework modeled both presence-absence (classification) and incidence (regression) to capture nonlinear interactions. Spatiotemporal dynamics were assessed using presence-absence data in a space-time permutation model fitted to a Poisson-type distribution and the Knox test. SCYLV presence-absence and incidence were significantly associated with varietal susceptibility, with CC 01-1940 and SP 71-6949 showing the highest case burden climatic variability, with major outbreak clusters coinciding with strong El Niño-Southern Oscillation anomalies of both phases (El Niño in 2007 and 2015/2016 and the strong 2010/2011 La Niña), meteorological conditions (temperature, wind speed, and vapor pressure), vegetation greenness (normalized difference vegetation index), and soil properties (pH, cation exchange capacity, sand content, and nitrogen). The Knox test identified a high-risk spatiotemporal window for SCYLV spread, with relative risk > 10 concentrated within 4.5 km and 4 days after a confirmed positive case, highlighting a critical range for targeted surveillance and rapid preventive action. This integrative workflow provides a reproducible, data-driven foundation for delineating differential risk zones and supports evidence-based strategies for the integrated management of SCYLV in Colombian sugarcane systems, with transferable methodological value for long-term surveillance analyses of other aphid-transmitted viruses.

Abstract
Plastic pollution is a growing concern. Plastic particles undergo aging that changes their properties and ecological impacts, yet how different polymers age in natural settings and relate to soil carbon cycling remains unclear. We conducted a nine-month in situ experiment to compare the aging of biodegradable PLA (polylactic acid) and conventional PVC (polyvinyl chloride) sheets and to assess their associations with soil microbial communities, enzymes, and organic carbon fractions. Abstract

Abstract
