Combined Application of Bamboo Biochar and Lime Alleviates Soil Acidification and Enhances Organic Carbon Stability in an Intensively Managed Acid Bamboo PlantationCORE8
Authors: Xuan Sun, Hao-Nan Ji, Lei Xu, Jia-Lin Wang, Xiao-Min Ma...
Venue: Agriculture
Published: 2026-10-28
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Liming is widely used to alleviate soil acidification, but its potential to stimulate soil organic carbon (SOC) decomposition may compromise SOC stability. Whether lime–biochar co-application can mitigate the associated increase in soil CO2 release remains unclear. A one-year field experiment in an intensively managed acid bamboo plantation compared an unamended control (CK), lime alone (L), bamboo biochar alone (B), and lime–biochar co-application (LB). Soils collected at 3, 6, and 12 months after application were analyzed for SOC fractions, extracellular enzyme activities, and microbial gene abundance and incubated for 90 days to measure CO2 release. At 3 and 6 months, L increased cumulative CO2-C release by 45% and 24%, respectively, relative to CK, accompanied by increased labile C and microbial C limitation inferred from enzyme stoichiometry. Relative to L, LB reduced mean CO2-C release rates by 18% and 13%, respectively; this response was associated with higher non-hydrolysable (recalcitrant) C, lower geometric mean enzyme activity, and weaker microbial C limitation. After 12 months, the stimulatory effect of L was no longer evident, whereas B and LB increased cumulative CO2-C release by 31% and 29%, respectively, relative to CK. Structural equation modeling linked the lime predictor to greater microbial abundance and C limitation, and the biochar predictor to higher recalcitrant C concentrations and lower C limitation. Because CO2 sources were not partitioned, these responses cannot be attributed exclusively to native SOC decomposition. Lime–biochar co-application thus moderated the short-term increase in soil CO2 release associated with liming, but this effect weakened over time, highlighting the need for longer-term assessment of SOC stability.

Optimizing vermicompost and inorganic fertilizer integration for enhancing bread wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) productivity and soil fertility in Northwestern EthiopiaCORE6
Authors: Abere Tenagne, Tadele Amare, Eyayu Molla
Venue: Discover Soil
Published: 2026-10-28

Abstract
Declining soil fertility is a major constraint to wheat production in the Ethiopian highlands, yet sufficient evidence on the combined effect of organic and inorganic nutrient sources on both soil fertility and wheat productivity remains limited under local conditions. Therefore, this study evaluated the effects of integrated application of vermicompost (VC) and nitrogen-phosphorus (N/P2O5) fertilizers on soil physicochemical properties and bread wheat productivity on Nitisols in Yilmana Densa district, Northwestern Ethiopia, during the 2025 main cropping season. A randomized complete block design with three replications was used in a 4 × 4 factorial arrangement of four VC rates (0, 3, 6, and 9 t ha− 1) and four N/P2O5 fertilizer levels (0/0, 46/46, 92/69, and 138/92 N/P2O5 kg ha− 1). Vermicompost significantly (p ≤ 0.05) improved soil pH, organic carbon, total nitrogen, available phosphorus, cation exchange capacity, and total porosity while reducing bulk density. Integrated VC and NP application also enhanced wheat growth, biomass, and grain yield at both experimental sites. The highest grain yields (5.0 and 5.6 t ha− 1) were obtained from 9 t VC ha− 1 plus 138/92 N/P2O5 kg ha− 1 at the two sites, respectively. This treatment achieved the highest net benefit of 306,208.5 Ethiopian Birr ha− 1 at Site 1 and 348,833.5 Ethiopian Birr ha− 1 at Site 2. Overall, integrated VC and N/P2O5 fertilization improved soil properties and wheat productivity under the conditions of this one-season study. Further multi-season and multi-location research is needed to assess the consistency, residual effect, and economic feasibility of these practices.

Plant Functional-Group Differentiation and Soil Property Variation Across Hummock–Hollow Microtopography in an Alpine Sedge Peatland on the Qinghai–Tibetan PlateauCORE3

Abstract
Hummock–hollow microtopography is associated with fine-scale variation in vegetation and soil properties in alpine peatlands. In this study, we compared plant diversity, aboveground biomass of functional groups, and soil properties between hummocks and hollows in five paired plots in an alpine sedge peatland on the Qinghai–Tibetan Plateau. Total aboveground biomass averaged 286.38 g m−2 in hollows and 228.32 g m−2 on hummocks. Hollows had greater sedge and grass biomass, whereas hummocks had greater forb biomass and higher plant diversity. Soil water content, total carbon, total nitrogen, nitrate nitrogen, and ammonium nitrogen were higher in hollows, whereas soil pH was higher in hummocks. Soil C:N and C:P ratios were lower in hummocks, whereas Olsen-P and N:P did not differ significantly between microforms. Pooled associations of functional-group biomass with plant diversity and soil properties largely paralleled these microform contrasts. These findings indicate that total aboveground biomass alone does not fully characterize vegetation differences between microforms and suggest that microtopographic variation should be considered when assessing vegetation and soil conditions in alpine peatlands.

Abstract
This study investigated the effects of different mixed−planting regimes of Idesia polycarpa on soil microbial community structure and diversity in the source region of the Huai River. Four planting systems were examined: pure forest, I. polycarpa + Camellia oleifera, I. polycarpa + C. oleifera + peanut, and I. polycarpa + peanut, designated as TB1, TB2, TB3, and TB4, respectively. Highthroughput sequencing was used to characterize changes in soil microbial composition and diversity among planting systems. The results showed that: (1) Mixed planting significantly altered soil microbial community structure. Compared with other treatments, TB4 exhibited significantly higher soil pH than TB1, TB2, and TB3 (p < 0.05), as well as higher− available potassium, alkali−hydrolyzable nitrogen, and organic matter contents than TB1 (p < 0.05). (2) The Chao and Shannon indices of soil microbial communities in TB3 were significantly higher than those in TB1, indicating enhanced microbial richness and diversity. (3) The RDA results showed that the overall model was not statistically significant (p = 0.496), indicating that no significant relationships between the measured soil environmental factors and bacterial community variation were identified in this study. Overall, TB3 exhibited relatively higher bacterial diversity, whereas TB4 showed a more favorable soil nutrient status. These findings suggest that soil properties and microbial community characteristics may vary among planting patterns. Future studies should expand sampling across multiple sites, increase sample sizes, and employ more robust statistical approaches to further investigate the relationships between planting patterns, soil properties, and microbial community characteristics.

Abstract
Nematodes are the most abundant metazoans and are widely used as indicators of ecological disturbance. We applied high-throughput sequencing (HTS) technology to explore the nematode communities in protected and unprotected areas of Mt. Kenya forest. The protected areas had a greater abundance of Rhabdolaimus (8.2%), Alaimus (7.7%), Eumonhystera (6.1%), Paratylenchus (3.4%), Teratocephalus (2.3%), Mylonchulus (2.1%), Mesodorylaimus (2%), Metateratocephalus (2%), Miconchus (1.8%), Tylenchulus (1.4%), Diphtherophora (1.4%) and Paractinolaimus (1.2%). Aphelenchus was more abundant in the unprotected site, which also had significantly lower Shannon index, number of observed amplicon sequence variants and Faith’s phylogenetic diversity. Beta diversity analysis revealed a clear differentiation of nematode assemblages in the two sites (PERMANOVA, P = 0.03). Soil pH and Fe had a significant effect (P<0.05) on abundance of nematode genera. Aphelenchus was positively associated with high pH while Mesodorylaimus, Diphtherophora and Paratylenchus showed a positive correlation with Fe. These findings demonstrate that forest protection enhances soil nematode diversity and community stability, highlighting the importance of conservation practices in maintaining belowground biodiversity and ecosystem functioning.

Abstract
Contaminated mining soils cause heavy metal environmental pollution such as the soil, river, groundwater and plants under rainfall conditions. In this work, the copper, zinc, lead and chromium leaching characteristics, leaching efficiency, fractions variation, binding intensity, mobility and potential environmental risks were investigated under different rainfall conditions. Results demonstrated that Zn obtained a high leaching concentration under the 19.6, 13.1 and 6.5 mL.min −1 , which concentrations were 180.3, 154.7 and 123.5 μg.L −1 , respectively. On the surface soil particles, Cu, Zn, Pb and Cr migrated into the water with the rainwater runoff, indicating a sustainable increase with rainfall intensity raising and obtained a high solubility and mobility, promoting heavy metals to migrate via the soil surface rainwater runoff. Exchangeable and reducible fractions of Pb and Cr have decreased and transformed into the metal ions, which migrated into the outflow, whereas the oxidizable and residual fractions have increased under the rainfall conditions. Cu and Pb obtained more stable fractions, such as the oxidizable and residual fractions. Cu, Zn, Pb and Cr I R values were affected by their concentration and fractions under the rainfall conditions. Heavy metal mobility order was Zn > Pb > Cr > Cu, with M F values of 4.34%, 3.52%, 2.67% and 2.45%, respectively. These results can provide significant support for the mining soils of efficient remediation methods to promote the mines' ecological environmental improvement.

Abstract

Abstract
To address the poor biodegradability and high environmental risks of traditional synthetic water-retaining agents (WRAs), and the vegetation colonization challenge induced by organic matter deficiency in saline-alkali mining areas, a biodegradable WRA (CCM−CMC) was facilely synthesized from natural cellulose (CCM) and carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) with epichlorohydrin (ECH) as a crosslinking agent. An in situ non-topsoil replacement remediation system of “superabsorbent polymer–biological fertilizer–native stress-tolerant plants” was established, and its water absorption-retention performance, biosafety, and ecological remediation efficacy were systematically evaluated. CCM−CMC displayed excellent equilibrium swelling ratios (1218, 443, 305, 148 g/g in distilled water, tap water, mining reclaimed water, and 0.9 wt% NaCl solution, respectively), with a 57.28% weight loss after 30-day soil burial, verifying favorable biodegradability. Soil application of CCM−CMC improved soil water-holding capacity by up to 39.20%, and maximally increased pakchoi germination rate, plant height, and dry weight by 23.33%, 327.4%, and 430.8% under drought stress. Field trials in Xinjiang arid mining areas achieved an over 80% survival rate for over ten native stress-tolerant plants, and significantly ameliorated the soil physicochemical properties. These eco-friendly WRAs and derived remediation technology avoid the high costs and ecological disturbance of topsoil replacement, providing a practical technical strategy for the ecological restoration of arid and highly saline-alkali mining areas.

Abstract
Jarosite-group minerals are ubiquitously observed in Martian sulfate-bearing deposits and serve as diagnostic proxies for past acidic aqueous environments. Proposed formation mechanisms include evaporation of acidic saline waters, magmatic vapor alteration, subglacial weathering, and microbially mediated Fe2+ oxidation. Among these, microbial formation has drawn astrobiological interest. To assess the effects of Martian atmospheric composition, we constructed a laboratory atmospheric simulation system and examined the Fe2+ oxidation and mineralization capacity of the acidophilic iron-oxidizing bacterium Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans under O2-depleted, CO2-rich conditions. Biomineralization experiments were performed across four CO2/O2 gas treatments, with initial dissolved O2 (DO) saturation values of 14.6%, 8.0%, 3.7%, and 1.4%, respectively. Fe2+ oxidation and jarosite precipitation were markedly delayed in the two lower-DO-saturation treatments, whereas biomineralization occurred in all inoculated treatments. XRD, SEM, EDS, and FTIR analyses revealed time-dependent mineralogical and compositional changes in the jarosite-group precipitates, with NH4-related features becoming more apparent during later mineralization. Abstract

Methods The rate of copper biosorption by the bacterial strain was examined using batch experiments in 100 ml of aqueous solution, in which separate biosorption tests were performed for temperature (20–45 °C), initial pH (4–9), copper concentration (10–240 g L−1), contact time (0-150 min), and biosorbent dose (0.25-5.0 g L−1).
