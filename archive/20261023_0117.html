Attention-Based Grouping Distance Stereo NetworkCORE4
Authors: Wen-Huan Wu, Fei Tang, Shuo Yang, Xi Xu
Venue: Electronics
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Stereo matching aims to accurately identify corresponding pixels and calculate the positional deviation between two corresponding pixels, which is of great significance to autonomous driving, 3D reconstruction, and other related tasks. Previous works rely on CNN-based feature extractors, lacking the means to utilize non-local context information and being easily affected by ill-posed regions. Additionally, the cost volumes of full correlation and concatenation lead to less necessary information and more parameters, respectively. In this paper, we propose the Attention-based Grouping Distance Stereo Network (AGDNet), which consists of three main modules: a Spatial Criss-Cross Attention module, Multi-head Channel Attention module, and grouping distance volume. The Spatial Criss-Cross Attention module constructs the long-distance-dependent relationship between pixels, which is propitious to making full use of non-local context information. The Multi-head Channel Attention module introduces multi-head attention strategy into the channel attention module, so as to obtain feature maps with a stronger capacity of representation. The grouping distance cost volume provides an efficient representation for measuring the feature similarity. It not only retains the available matching cues in the feature vectors, but also reduces the size of cost volume. Experimental results that evaluated the Scene Flow dataset, KITTI 2015 dataset, and KITTI 2012 dataset demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance.
LonGaussian: Tracking Foundation Priors for Long-Context Feedforward Gaussian ReconstructionCORE4
Authors: Zhou-Feng Xie, Guan-Jun Wu, Shuai Zhang, Xing-Gang Wang, Wen-Yu Liu
Venue: Artificial Intelligence Review
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs
Abstract
Reconstructing high-quality, renderable 3D scenes from long videos is essential for robotic perception and embodied intelligence. Many feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction methods rapidly predict 3D scenes from a small set of images, but extending them to longer sequences presents two bottlenecks: jointly processing all views increases GPU memory requirements, while accumulating pixel-aligned predictions redundantly represents the same surfaces and increases storage and rendering costs. We present LonGaussian, a streaming feed-forward framework that reconstructs compact, globally renderable Gaussian scenes from long image sequences with known intrinsics and unknown camera poses. Built on a pretrained streaming 3D reconstruction backbone, LonGaussian decodes long-context geometric features into dense Gaussians. Within each chunk, multi-frame point tracks link repeated observations of the same surface and guide a learned merger to consolidate the corresponding Gaussian predictions. Rendered-depth consistency filtering further suppresses Gaussians from other chunks that incorrectly occlude locally reconstructed surfaces. On DL3DV and RealEstate10K benchmarks, LonGaussian improves novel-view rendering quality while using substantially fewer Gaussians than the evaluated feed-forward baselines.
An algorithm for 6D object pose estimation based on generative single-image 3D reconstructionCORE3
Authors: J. Lou, S. Shavetov, N. Tang
Venue: Journal of Instrument Engineering
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Reliable 6D object pose estimation is a foundational capability for robotic manipulation in Industry 4.0 and unstructured warehouse environments. Current state-of-the-art methods, such as PVN3D and SAM-6D, predominantly rely on “Model-Based” paradigms that necessitate high-fidelity CAD models of the target objects for training or template matching. This requirement severely limits their applicability in open-world scenarios where such CAD models are unavailable in real-time. To bridge this gap, a novel 6D Pose Estimation Framework without fine-tuning on the target object (Zero-Shot) is proposed to eliminate the dependency on prior CAD models. A single-view 3D generative model is leveraged to synthesize a geometrically plausible 3D mesh from a single RGB image. To address the scale ambiguity inherent in generative models, which poses a critical challenge for robotic grasping, a Template-Prior Iterative Closest Point (TP-ICP) based on the classical ICP is introduced for iterative recovery of the metric scale. This algorithm robustly recovers the metric physical scale of the neural-generated mesh using depth information, employing median-based outlier removal and template-guided ICP to handle occlusions and sensor noise. The recovered metric mesh is then seamlessly integrated into a standard 6D pose estimation pipeline, establishing a closed-loop framework comprising model self-generation, metric scale calibration, and pose estimation. A fully reproducible implementation scheme is presented. Systematic experiments on the LINEMOD dataset demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves robust 6D pose estimation suitable for robotic tasks, even in the complete absence of prior CAD models.

Abstract
Recent progress in 3D generation has motivated growing interest in compact generative representations of complex 3D scenes. For native 3D Gaussian scenes, however, learning such representations remains challenging due to widely varying primitive counts, nonuniform spatial density, and differences in geometry and appearance even among spatially neighboring primitives. In this work, we introduce TriFold, a native 3D Gaussian VAE that encodes Gaussian scenes into a fixed-layout triplane latent and jointly reconstructs their geometry and appearance. The key insight is to impose structural regularity on the latent space while preserving the spatial adaptivity of the underlying Gaussians. During encoding, we use cell-wise learned queries to aggregate projected Gaussians into multiple slots per cell, reducing the information loss caused by single-feature pooling. During decoding, occupancy prediction identifies active regions, where query-based set prediction and candidate selection reconstruct local Gaussian sets with spatially varying primitive counts. Building on this representation, we train a latent flow-matching model for conditional scene-level 3DGS generation, enabling point-cloud-conditioned synthesis and single-image-conditioned amodal generation. Landmark-Free and Scale-Blind Multi-View Registration of Preoperative Liver Models to Intraoperative SurfacesPROXY2
Authors: Jia-Ming Feng, G. Galindo, Shahid Farid, Sharib Ali
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs
Abstract
Augmented-reality guidance in laparoscopic liver resection requires aligning preoperative 3D liver models from CT/MRI with intraoperative surfaces. Monocular reconstruction in most cases cannot guarantee scale, albeit rigid-motion registration requires scale as an input. Our proposed framework ``SimCompose''estimates the full similarity transformation $\Sim$, including scale, without relying on landmarks and applies the same transformation across multiple views with known relative geometry. However, reliable detection of registration failures remains challenging in surgical settings, where ground-truth alignment is generally unavailable. For this, we propose a geometry-based selector that evaluates candidate transformations without requiring ground truth. Large disagreement among these transformations is used to flag potentially unreliable registrations. Finally, we propose to use a non-rigid refinement based on a low-dimensional deformation model from Neo-Hookean simulations in the Simulation Open Framework Architecture (SOFA), without additional network training. SimCompose is trained exclusively on synthetic partial views. Its encoder processes point clouds normalised to a unit root-mean-square radius, without access to metric scale, and filters each view before multi-view fusion. Evaluation on unseen synthetic data (300 single-view pairs per run; 100 three-view registrations) shows that multi-view integration and candidate selection increase the success rate under stringent $\Sim$ accuracy criteria from 38.4% for a single-view, single-method configuration to 77%. Existing learning-based baselines do not satisfy these criteria even when provided with the exact scale. On five in-vivo patients with 92 keyframes, the rigid registration stage reduces surface error more than three-fold relative to learning-based baselines with external scale information.

Abstract
Mainstream point cloud completion methods are unanimously based on deep learning architectures. On one hand, these methods tend to repetitively stacking attention modules to enhance network performance, which drifts away from the neat design of unified and interpret-able networks; on the other hand, nearly all existing architectures rely solely on feed-forward connections to propagate information, neglecting feedback from deeper layers. The recurrent neural architecture, formed by feedforward-feedback loops, have shown its ability to generate Illusory Contours (ICs) in the V1 brain region of mammals; and ICs are essentially a completion mechanism of the brain for visual information. Motivated by this, we design a Brain-inspired Recurrent Illusory Contour Encoder (BRICE) to"imagine"what is missing in the incomplete 3D point clouds. Building upon the foundation model of Predictive Coding (PC), the plug-and-play nature enables BRICE to be seamlessly integrated into mainstream point cloud completion baselines. Authors: N. Nipu, G. Johnson, C. Roth, D. Rogers, S. Starkenburg...
Venue: Pattern Recognition
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs
Abstract
3D genome structures reconstructed from Hi-C data provide valuable insights into chromatin organization, but comparing them across conditions and time points remains challenging because of noise, variability, and arbitrary orientation. State-of-the-art comparison approaches often rely on contact-matrix-based summaries, which do not preserve the spatial geometry of the reconstructed structures and make integration with gene expression and chromatin accessibility difficult. There is therefore a need for robust structural alignment and spatial abstraction methods that support integrated analysis across conditions and time points. We present MultiPASE, a computational pipeline for comparative analysis of reconstructed 3D genome structures represented as gene-level spatial point clouds. MultiPASE aligns point clouds into a shared coordinate frame using coarse-to-fine rigid registration with PCA-based axis disambiguation and robust ICP refinement, then summarizes spatial organization using two complementary shape abstraction methods that produce stable, nested, multi-scale representations from dense core regions to broader structural extent. Using Hi-C-derived reconstructions from viral infection experiments, we show how the approach reveals condition-dependent structural changes that are difficult to interpret from raw 3D structures alone. By integrating spatial structure with gene expression and chromatin accessibility, MultiPASE supports interactive analysis of spatial organization and gene relocation patterns. A quantitative validation, reported in the Supplementary Materials, shows that the proposed shape abstractions preserve non-convex features, remain stable across samples, and that the alignment pipeline is necessary for meaningful comparison. A usage case and a usability evaluation with eight participants demonstrate the pipeline's effectiveness for comparative structural analysis.
Relation-aware dynamic knowledge subgraph enhanced large language models for medical multiple-choice question answeringECO1

Venue: PLOS One
Published: 2026-10-16

Abstract
Medical multiple-choice question answering requires models to simultaneously understand question semantics, candidate-answer differences, and structured relations among medical entities in complex clinical contexts. To address the limitations that existing knowledge-enhanced methods often adopt question-level unified modeling and thus struggle to characterize local relational differences among different candidate options, while conventional low-rank adaptation lacks relation-pattern adaptivity, this paper proposes a knowledge-enhanced large language model framework that combines option-level knowledge subgraph representation fusion with relation-aware dynamic LoRA adaptation. The proposed method first constructs a local medical knowledge subgraph for the question stem and each candidate answer separately, and then injects structured knowledge into candidate representations through a graph encoder and gated fusion mechanism. Subsequently, multiple learned LoRA expert branches are dynamically combined through candidate-conditioned routing based on the relational information encoded in the enhanced candidate representations, enabling the model to obtain more suitable parameter update paths. The experimental results are reported as the mean and standard deviation over three random-seed runs. On the MedQA dataset, the proposed method achieves Accuracy scores of 62.90%±0.82% and 62.84%±1.04%, together with F1-Score values of 62.97%±1.07% and 62.79%±0.78%, using Llama3 8B and Qwen 7B, respectively. On the MedMCQA dataset, the corresponding Accuracy scores are 61.04%±0.30% and 58.59%±0.65%, while the F1-Score values reach 61.21%±0.54% and 57.77%±0.57%, respectively. Experimental results show that the proposed method can effectively improve knowledge understanding, candidate discrimination, and reasoning stability in medical multiple-choice question answering.
Context presentation and native conversation structure in large language model responses to escalating suicide risk: a controlled multimodel evaluationECO1
Authors: Jue-Qiao Liu, Xiao-Yi Chen, Jun Cai, Xu-Chen Luo, Wei Xiao...
Venue: Frontiers in Psychiatry
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Large language models are increasingly used for informal mental health support, yet their safety during escalating suicide-risk conversations may depend on how prior information is presented. Native multi-turn conversations and reconstructed cumulative prompts differ not only in the presence of prior model responses but also in prompt reconstruction, information concentration, message positioning, explicit integration cues, and conversation-state processing. This study compared suicide-risk recognition, direct safety inquiry, response urgency, and timely real-world intervention across isolated current-turn prompts, reconstructed cumulative user-only prompts, and native continuous multi-turn conversations. We conducted a controlled repeated-measures evaluation of ChatGPT, Gemini, and Doubao using 12 standardized five-turn trajectories: 4 direct suicide-risk trajectories, 4 implicit or internally inconsistent risk trajectories, and 4 non-suicidal high-distress controls. Each trajectory was evaluated under three context-presentation formats. The 24 model-trajectory combinations were treated as matched repeated-measures units nested within 8 independent risk-trajectory blocks. Primary contrasts were estimated using exact trajectory-level sign-flip tests and trajectory-block bootstrap confidence intervals. Turn-level outcomes were analyzed using multilevel mixed-effects models. Exploratory condition-by-model heterogeneity was assessed using a trajectory-blocked exact permutation sensitivity analysis, with a trajectory-clustered interaction model used only as a secondary asymptotic check. Timely appropriate intervention occurred in 10/24 isolated current-turn observations (41.7%), 21/24 reconstructed cumulative observations (87.5%), and 12/24 native continuous observations (50.0%). On average across the three systems, reconstructed cumulative presentation exceeded isolated input by 45.8 percentage points (95% trajectory-block bootstrap CI, 25.0 to 66.7; Holm-adjusted exact P = .031), whereas native continuous performance was 37.5 percentage points lower than reconstructed cumulative performance (95% trajectory-block bootstrap CI, −50.0 to −25.0; Holm-adjusted exact P = .031). Exploratory model-specific analyses indicated substantial heterogeneity: the native-versus-reconstructed contrast was largest for Doubao, smaller and statistically imprecise for Gemini, and absent for ChatGPT. On average across the three evaluated systems, reconstructed cumulative user-only prompts outperformed native continuous conversations, although exploratory model-specific patterns were heterogeneous. Because the formats also differed in prompt reconstruction, information salience, message positioning, explicit integration instructions, and native conversation-state processing, the contrast cannot isolate the causal effect of prior model responses.
