GIS-BASED ENVIRONMENTAL RISK ASSESSMENT IN INDUSTRIAL CITIES: METHODOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK AND APPLICATION TO ALMALYK, UZBEKISTANECO1
Authors: Adilov Zarif Himmatovich, Ziad Baydoun, Oripova Sarvinoz Abduqaxor qizi, Ubaydullakhujaev Saidazizkhuja Utkur ugli, Mamazhanova Zukhra Alizhon kizi
Venue: PLANNING MALAYSIA
Published: 2026-08-09
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Industrial cities are characterized by the coexistence of intensive production, dense settlement patterns, transport infrastructure, and long-term anthropogenic pressure on environmental systems. Under such conditions, environmental risk is not confined to a single medium but emerges through the interaction of atmospheric pollution, groundwater and soil contamination, ecological degradation, and human exposure. This study develops a geoinformation-based methodological framework for assessing environmental risks in industrial urban territories and applies it to Almalyk, Uzbekistan, as a representative mining and metallurgical city. The framework integrates remote sensing, spatial interpolation, buffer analysis, thematic layer overlay, and multi-criteria evaluation within a unified GIS environment. Environmental indicators related to air quality, groundwater contamination, land use, vegetation condition, industrial proximity, and population vulnerability are standardized and combined to derive a composite environmental risk index. The results show that environmental burden in Almalyk is unevenly distributed and is concentrated in industrial zones and adjoining residential territories, especially those influenced by mining and metallurgical activity, waste disposal areas, and pollutant transport pathways. The resulting risk map provides a practical analytical basis for environmental monitoring, urban planning, zoning control, and public health protection in industrial cities.

Spatiotemporal Agricultural Drought Dynamics in the Chi River Basin, Thailand: A Google Earth Engine-Based Multi-Criteria AssessmentECO1
Authors: N. Homtong, Jirawat Kasmanee
Venue: Earth
Published: 2026-08-09
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Agricultural drought threatens rainfed agriculture in northeast Thailand, where variable monsoon rainfall, limited irrigation access, and extensive cropland increase vulnerability. This study developed a Google Earth Engine-based Agricultural Drought Risk Index (ADRI) for the Chi River Basin using six benchmark years (2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020, and 2025). CHIRPS precipitation, MODIS-derived vegetation health, ERA5-Land soil moisture, irrigation accessibility, and agricultural land exposure were normalized and integrated by weighted linear combination. The analysis quantified risk-class areas, irrigated–rainfed contrasts, persistent hotspots, weight sensitivity, and spatial agreement with the official Land Development Department recurring-drought map. Moderate risk dominated most years, but high-risk area expanded to 60.7% in 2015, coincident with severe rainfall deficits during the 2015–2016 El Niño event. Conditions improved in 2020 and 2025 as rainfall, vegetation health, and soil moisture recovered. Rainfed areas consistently had higher ADRI values than irrigated areas, and persistent hotspots were concentrated in southeastern and downstream agricultural zones. The principal spatial and temporal patterns remained stable under ±10% weight perturbations. External validation identified ADRI > 2.90 as the optimal threshold, with raster-level precision, recall, and F1 of 0.779, 0.884, and 0.828, respectively; the 998-point sample produced an F1 of 0.832. ADRI therefore provides a practical basin-scale screening framework for drought monitoring, adaptation prioritization, and agricultural water-management planning.
