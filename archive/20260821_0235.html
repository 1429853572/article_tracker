Effects of AB Mix hydroponic solution and ameliorant mixtures on cayenne pepper growth and yield in an InceptisolsCORE6
Authors: Tien Turmuktini, Betty Natalie Fitriatin, T. Simarmata, Firda Widayanti, Nuryanti Nuryanti...

Abstract
Cayenne pepper (Capsicum frustescens L.) cultivation faces several problems, including acidic pH and inadequate nutrient management. These problems can be addressed by using a mixture of various ameliorants and AB mix nutrient solutions. This study aimed to determine the effect of ameliorant mixture dosage and AB mix solution concentration on soil chemical properties, growth, and yield of cayenne pepper. The experiment used a randomized block design, consisting of two factors: nutrient solution concentration and ameliorant dosage. The factor of nutrient solution concentration consisted of four levels, i.e., 700, 1000, 1300, and 1600 ppm, while the factor of ameliorant dosage also consisted of four levels, i.e., 0, 2, 4, and 6 t ha-1. Observations were made on soil chemical properties, namely soil pH, organic carbon content, and cation exchange capacity (CEC). The agronomic characteristics observed were plant height, number of leaves, leaf area, chlorophyll content index, fruit length, number of fruits, and fruit weight per plant. The results showed no interaction between the concentration of AB mix solution and the ameliorant dosage on soil chemical properties, growth, and yield characteristics of cayenne pepper plants. The single effect of AB mix solution or ameliorant was observed on the number and weight of fruits per plant. The optimal dose for achieving the maximum harvested fruit weight was 6 t ha-1 of the ameliorant mixture (76.55 g pepper/plant), while the optimal AB mix concentration was 1600 ppm (65.02 g pepper/plant).
Summary / 总结
Cayenne pepper production on Inceptisols is limited by acidic soil pH and poor nutrient management, prompting research into combining ameliorant mixtures with AB mix hydroponic solution. A randomized block experiment tested four AB mix concentrations (700, 1000, 1300, 1600 ppm) and four ameliorant dosages (0, 2, 4, 6 t ha-1), measuring soil pH, organic carbon, cation exchange capacity, and plant growth and yield traits. No significant interaction was found between the two factors, but each individually affected fruit number and weight per plant. The highest fruit weight per plant was achieved with 6 t ha-1 ameliorant (76.55 g) and with 1600 ppm AB mix (65.02 g).

Assessment of Heavy Metal Concentrations and Geochemical Speciation in Dumpsite Soils Derived from Coastal Plain Sands in Akwa Ibom State, NigeriaCORE6
Authors: G. E. Essien
Venue: Journal of Agriculture and Ecology Research International
Published: 2026-08-14
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study assessed the concentrations and geochemical speciation of Pb, Cd, Cr, Ni, and V in dumpsite soils derived from coastal plain sands in Akwa Ibom State, Nigeria. Automechanic, paint-processing, and abattoir dumpsites were investigated in Etinan, Uyo, and Ikot Ekpene, with uncontaminated soils serving as controls. Soil samples were collected from 0–20, 20–40, and 40–60 cm depths and analysed for physicochemical properties, total metal concentrations, and six operationally defined geochemical fractions using sequential extraction and atomic absorption spectrophotometry. Metal mobility and contamination indices were also evaluated. Waste deposition altered soil properties, with dumpsite soils generally showing higher pH, organic matter, exchangeable bases, and base saturation than control soils. Heavy metal concentrations were markedly elevated at all affected sites, particularly in automechanic and paint-processing soils. Lead and cadmium occurred in appreciable proportions in water-soluble, exchangeable, and carbonate-bound fractions, indicating comparatively greater potential mobility and bioavailability. Chromium, nickel, and vanadium were more strongly associated with reducible and residual fractions, although measurable proportions occurred in non-residual forms. The results demonstrate that waste type influenced both metal accumulation and geochemical partitioning. While stable fractions accounted for part of the metal burden, the occurrence of metals in labile fractions indicates potential for leaching, plant uptake, and broader environmental exposure. Improved waste segregation, controlled disposal, and periodic soil monitoring are therefore required in the study area.

Abstract
Abstract— The sorption capacity and strength of fixation of Cu(II) and Ni(II) by organic and mineral horizons of soils of the Norilsk industrial region have been studied in laboratory experiments with podbur, cryoturbated peat gleyzem, raw-humus cryozem, and cryometamorphic gleyzem (Spodic Cryosol, Histic Gleyic Cryosol, Turbic Cryosol, and Gleyic Cryosol). The soils were taken at key plots located at a distance of 7 to 10 km from the city of Norilsk in the western and northwestern directions. The studied soils have a high sorption capacity to Cu(II) and Ni(II). Under conditions of artificial soil contamination with sulfate solutions at concentrations ranging from 0.5 to 25 mg/L and the soil-to-solution ratio of 1 : 20, 1 : 20, the degree of extraction of metal ions ranged between 80 and 100%. The main mechanisms of adsorption in most studied horizons in the absence of competition between Cu(II) and Ni(II) are ionic chemisorption and ion exchange (the adsorption energy G varies from 8 to 13 kJ/mol). It has been found that Cu(II) is absorbed by the soil more actively and more firmly than Ni(II), which was especially pronounced in organic horizons. All the studied soils, when < 50 mg/kg of Cu(II) and Ni(II) were added to them, firmly retained the absorbed metal ions. Taking into account the significant sorption capacity of the studied soils and the ability of soils to fix firmly Cu(II) and Ni(II) in a certain range of metal concentrations, it has been suggested that it would be appropriate to consider the feasibility of setting standards for the content of heavy metals in soils based on the content of their water-soluble forms, taking into account the pH, cation exchange capacity, and Corg content of the soil.

Land-Use and Depth-Dependent Assembly of Soil Microbiomes Shapes Ecological Functions, Interaction Networks, and Phytopathogenic Communities Across Crop and Orchard SystemsCORE4

Venue: Antioxidants
Published: 2026-08-14

Abstract
Soil microorganisms are essential for nutrient cycling, plant productivity, and soil health, yet the relative importance of land-use and soil depth in shaping agricultural microbiomes remains poorly understood. This study investigated soil microbial communities across uncultivated land, alfalfa fields, crop systems (feed corn and sweet corn), and orchard systems (walnut and quince) in the Hajdúnánás region of Hungary using shotgun metagenomic sequencing and soil physicochemical analyses. Microbial alpha diversity showed limited variation among land-use systems but declined significantly with soil depth in both bacterial (Kruskal–Wallis, p = 0.00054) and fungal (p = 0.00051) communities. Beta diversity analyses identified soil depth as the primary driver of microbial community composition in both bacterial (R2 = 0.305, p = 0.001) and fungal (R2 = 0.277, p = 0.001) communities. In contrast, land-use significantly influenced only fungal community composition (R2 = 0.250, p = 0.005). Fungal alpha diversity showed significant negative relationships with soil pH and CaCO3, whereas bacterial diversity exhibited only weak correlations. Crop soils contained the highest numbers of unique bacterial and fungal taxa. Functional analyses revealed significant differences in nutrient cycling, plant-growth-related, decomposition, and environmental adaptation functions among land-use systems. In crop soils, topsoil communities were enriched in oxidative stress-related pathways involved in reactive oxygen species detoxification (ROS), redox homeostasis, and stress regulation, whereas subsoil communities showed a greater representation of antioxidant metabolite production functions. Co-occurrence network analyses indicated greater connectivity in perennial systems, particularly alfalfa soils. Analyses of bacterial and fungal species with reported phytopathogenic potential identified stable cores of phytopathogenic species across agricultural systems, with soil pH emerging as the strongest environmental factor associated with the abundance of phytopathogenic species. Overall, soil depth was the primary driver of bacterial and fungal community assembly, whereas land-use primarily shaped fungal community composition and influenced ecological functions, microbial interaction networks, and the distribution of phytopathogenic species.
Summary / 总结
This study aimed to clarify how land-use type and soil depth jointly shape agricultural soil microbiomes and their ecological functions, using shotgun metagenomic sequencing and soil physicochemical analyses across uncultivated land, alfalfa, crop, and orchard systems in Hungary. Microbial alpha diversity varied little among land-use systems but declined significantly with soil depth for both bacteria and fungi, while beta diversity analyses identified depth as the primary driver of community composition and land-use as a significant factor only for fungal communities. Functional profiles differed among land-use systems, with crop topsoils enriched in oxidative stress and redox regulation pathways and subsoils showing greater representation of antioxidant metabolite production functions. Co-occurrence networks were more connected in perennial systems, and stable cores of phytopathogenic species were detected across agricultural systems, with soil pH emerging as the strongest environmental correlate of their abundance.

Abstract
Abstract In terrestrial ecosystems, soil is the primary medium that accumulates polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). In this study, the distribution of PAHs in soils of landscapes remote from major pollution sources is examined. A loamy soil-geochemical catena was studied on the territory of the Central Forest Reserve, in the southern part of the Valdai Hills. Soil pH, organic carbon content, CaCO3, particle-size distribution, and concentrations of 12 PAHs: naphthalene, fluorene, phenanthrene, anthracene, fluoranthene, pyrene, benz[a]anthracene, benzo[b]fluoranthene, benzo[k]fluoranthene, benzo[a]pyrene, dibenz[ah]anthracene, benzo[ghi]perylene were determined. The summit and upper slope positions of the catena are covered with coniferous-broadleaf forests on Retisols. The footslope position is covered by spruce forest on Stagnosol. The total concentration of 12 PAHs is comparable to that of forest soils of Central Europe and northern European Russia. The leading factors in the vertical distribution of PAHs in soil profile are the organic matter content, particle size, and acidity. The PAH concentration is highest in the topsoils (1354–2686 ng/g), where low molecular weight PAHs associated with organic matter predominate. In the Bt horizon, low molecular weight PAHs are predominantly sorbed by solid particles smaller than 10 μm in diameter. A decrease in the organic matter content, accompanied by an increase in pH, contributes to a decrease in the PAH concentration in the soil profile with depth. The total PAHs concentration in topsoil is higher in the subordinate positions due to slower decomposition of coniferous litter saturated with resinous substances and higher stability of PAHs under anaerobic conditions.
Summary / 总结
The motivation was to understand background levels and distribution of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in soils remote from major pollution sources, since soil is the main terrestrial sink for PAHs. The study examined a loamy soil-geochemical catena in the Central Forest Reserve in the southern Valdai Hills, measuring soil pH, organic carbon, CaCO3, particle-size distribution, and concentrations of 12 PAHs in Retisols under coniferous-broadleaf forest and a Stagnosol under spruce forest. Total PAH concentrations in topsoils ranged from 1354 to 2686 ng/g, comparable to forest soils of Central Europe and northern European Russia, with low-molecular-weight PAHs predominating and associated with organic matter. In the Bt horizon, low-molecular-weight PAHs were mainly sorbed by particles smaller than 10 μm, while decreasing organic matter and increasing pH led to lower PAH concentrations with depth. Higher topsoil PAH totals in subordinate landscape positions were attributed to slower decomposition of resinous coniferous litter and greater PAH stability under anaerobic conditions.

Abstract
Organic phosphorus (OP) transformation in perennial orchard soils is strongly regulated by planting duration. However, the pathways of soil OP turnover in the orchards with different planting years remain largely unknown. This study was conducted in Penglai District, Shandong Province, China, characterized by a warm-temperate continental monsoon climate and brown sandy loam soil. Soil samples were collected from the 0–20 cm layer of vineyards established for 0.5, 4, 16, and 22 years (Y0.5, Y4, Y16, and Y22, respectively). Soil P pools, OP forms determined by solution 31P nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, acid and alkaline phosphomonoesterase (ACP and ALP), phosphodiesterase (PDE) activities, and the diversity and composition of phoC- and phoD-harboring bacterial communities were analyzed. Differences among vineyard ages were assessed using one-way ANOVA, while principal coordinate analysis, canonical correlation analysis, and structural equation modeling were employed to evaluate microbial community variation and the relationships among soil properties, functional bacterial communities, phosphatase activities, and OP forms. We observed that compared with that in Y0.5, total P content in Y4 was significantly lower, whereas available P (AP) content in Y16, and OP content in both Y16 and Y22, were significantly higher. The contents of orthophosphate, myo-inositol hexakisphosphate (myo-IHP), scyllo-IHP, choline phosphate and corrected monoester (cMonoester) in Y16 and Y22 were significantly higher than that in Y0.5. Soil ACP activity in Y16 was significantly higher than that in Y4, but ALP and PDE activities in Y16 were significantly lower than those in other planting years. The phoC- and phoD-harboring community composition in Y16 was significantly altered, with phoC- and phoD-harboring community α-diversity remarkably increased and decreased, respectively. The phoC- and phoD-harboring community composition was driven by soil pH, AP and cMonoester, with phoD-harboring community composition predicted by the soil C:P ratio. Soil OP transformation is mainly regulated by the activity of PDE and ACP, and the diversity and composition of phoC-harboring communities, in orchards with different planting years.
Summary / 总结
The motivation was to clarify how vineyard planting duration affects soil organic phosphorus turnover, since the pathways of organic phosphorus transformation in perennial orchards of different ages remain poorly understood. The study analyzed 0–20 cm soil samples from vineyards established for 0.5, 4, 16, and 22 years in Penglai, Shandong, China, using solution 31P nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, phosphatase activity assays, and phoC- and phoD-harboring bacterial community analysis, with statistical approaches including ANOVA, principal coordinate analysis, canonical correlation analysis, and structural equation modeling. Key results showed that compared with the youngest vineyard, total phosphorus decreased at 4 years, while available phosphorus at 16 years and organic phosphorus at both 16 and 22 years increased significantly; orthophosphate, myo-inositol hexakisphosphate, scyllo-inositol hexakisphosphate, choline phosphate, and corrected monoester also increased in older vineyards. Acid phosphomonoesterase activity peaked at 16 years, while alkaline phosphomonoesterase and phosphodiesterase activities were lowest at 16 years; phoC-harboring community diversity increased and phoD-harboring community diversity decreased at that age, with community composition driven by soil pH, available phosphorus, and corrected monoester. The study concluded that organic phosphorus turnover in vineyards of differing ages is mainly regulated by phosphodiesterase and acid phosphomonoesterase activities and by the diversity and composition of phoC-harboring bacterial communities.

Effects of Seasonal Bush Burning on Indigenous Woody Plant Species Seed Germination and Seedling Survival in Kogi-West, North-Central NigeriaCORE3
Authors: F. Owa

Published: 2026-08-14

Abstract
This study evaluated the influence of seasonal bush burning on seed germination, seedling development, survival, and selected soil physicochemical properties of thirty indigenous woody species in Kogi-West, Nigeria. Seasonal fire remains one of the most pervasive ecological disturbances influencing vegetation dynamics in tropical savanna ecosystems. Despite its widespread occurrence across North-Central Nigeria, empirical information on how bush burning affects the regenerative capacity of indigenous woody species remains limited. The species were grouped into fire-tolerant, moderately fire-tolerant, and non-fire-tolerant categories and monitored under burnt and unburnt field conditions for six months using a randomized complete block design. Data were collected on germination percentage, mean germination time, seedling height, leaf production, survival rate, soil pH, organic matter content, available phosphorus, and ash depth. The findings revealed marked differences among fire tolerance groups. Fire-tolerant species recorded superior performance in burnt plots, with mean germination, seedling height, and survival values of 78.6%, 35.5 cm, and 82.5%, respectively. Conversely, non-fire-tolerant species exhibited substantial declines in germination (33.8%), growth (14.7 cm), and survival (38.8%) following fire exposure. Soil analyses showed elevated pH and phosphorus levels in burnt plots but significant reductions in organic matter. The study demonstrates that seasonal bush burning functions as an ecological filter that favors fire adapted woody species while constraining recruitment of fire-sensitive taxa. Continued annual burning may therefore accelerate shifts in species composition, reduce floristic diversity, and undermine ecosystem resilience. The study recommends integrated fire management, restoration of degraded sites, and targeted conservation of vulnerable indigenous woody species to sustain biodiversity and ecological stability within the Guinea Savanna of Kogi-West.
Summary / 总结

Abstract
Abstract In regions where hydrocarbons are extracted, there remains a challenge in identifying uncontaminated (notionally background) land plots, necessary for developing soil quality standards with respect to petroleum hydrocarbon (PH) content and assessing the level of soil contamination by petroleum. This paper examines approaches to diagnosing the hydrocarbon status of soils at oil fields. The composition and profile distribution of normal alkanes (n-alkanes) as biomarkers of transformation of hydrocarbons, including petroleum products, in soils of the tundra zone, northern taiga, and dry and dry steppe zones are studied. Changes in the composition and profile distribution of n-alkanes in oil-contaminated soils compared to background soils are identified, and the possibility for identifying self-purification processes based on the results of PH composition assessment is demonstrated. Homologues C25, C27, C29, and C31 predominate in the composition of n-alkanes in soils of the tundra and taiga zones; their genesis is associated with the decarboxylation of fatty acids entering the soil with plant residues. In the soils of the dry steppe zone, the predominant ones are n-alkanes of the ligroin-kerosene fraction with a characteristic low proportion of high-molecular homologues. Estimated coefficients based on the composition of n-alkanes (∑C15C17C19, ∑(C20–C25), ∑(C26–C40)) allow us to judge about the rate of self-purification of oil-contaminated soils. The intensity of n-alkane transformation has been shown to be statistically significantly associated with soil properties, natural and climatic factors, as well as the composition of crude oil and the level of oil contamination. When selecting background analogues of oil-contaminated soils in tundra and taiga, it is advisable to take into account the nature of the distribution of even and odd homologues of n-alkanes, the value of the sum of odd n-alkanes (ΣC27C29C31) along with the total content of PH, when selecting background analogues of oil-contaminated tundra and taiga soils. Abstract
Biochar is widely applied in soils to improve environmental quality. However, reported biochar effects remain highly variable and often contradictory, even under similar experimental conditions. This inconsistency is commonly attributed to differences in feedstock, pyrolysis conditions, and soil properties. A fundamental limitation persists as biochar is usually applied on a mass basis, despite large variability in the functional properties that govern its environmental performance. Equal masses of different biochar do not deliver equivalent functional capacity. This perspective introduces the Principle of Functional Delivery (PoFuD), which reframes biochar application from a mass-based to a function-based. PoFuD proposes that biochar effects are determined by the type and quantity of functional capacity delivered rather than by the applied mass. This article outlines a functional standardisation approach in which biochar application is normalised to a target property (or process-relevant function), while all co-delivered properties are measured transparently and treated as contextual background. Adopting PoFuD would clarify dose-response relationships, improve comparability across studies, and strengthen mechanistic interpretation. This conceptual shift has broad implications for soil remediation, biogeochemistry, greenhouse gas mitigation, and policy-relevant biochar research, and provides a foundation for more robust experimental design and standardisation in environmental applications.

Abstract
Abstract— The objective of the study was to provide the scientific basis for elaborating soil quality standards for soils affected by the impact of a waste disposal facility produced by tin-containing ore processing. The standards should be based on a macrokinetic model of biological growth and on an integrated indicator of soil condition.
