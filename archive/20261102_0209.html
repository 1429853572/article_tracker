SceneAgent: An Agentic Pipeline from 3D and 4D Captures to Simulation EnvironmentsCORE8
Authors: Luke Hollis, Tian-Xing Fan, Heng Yang
Venue: Science Robotics
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs
Abstract
Visual captures provide detailed scene appearance, but converting them into interactive simulation environments requires object reconstruction, physical modeling, and correction of intermediate errors. We present SceneAgent, an agentic pipeline that coordinates these steps through simulator-based visual inspection and repair. The pipeline processes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), photogrammetry, LiDAR captures, AI-generated 3DGS scenes, and 4D videos of dynamic scenes, and combines object segmentation, physical parameter estimation, part decomposition, articulation, and deformable simulation. To support policy learning, SceneAgent generates diverse object variants ("digital sisters") and scripted demonstrations. On a cluttered bin scene from GraspClutter6D, SceneAgent raises F1 at 1 cm for every coding agent with scorable outputs both with and without its skills, and its reconstructions are better than SimFoundry: all agent configurations exceed SimFoundry's F1 score, with a best of 60.75% versus 6.9%. On three dynamic scenes from 4DCodeBench, SceneAgent raises the mean dynamic IoU of Gemini 4 Argon from 0.86% to 99.09%. We further demonstrate diverse simulated interactions and preliminary policy transfer to a real robot.

Generative Structure from Motion with Native 3D DiffusionCORE7
Authors: Hao-Bin Duan, Binbin Huang, M. Xiang, Yiqun Zhao, Zi-Bo Zhao...
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs
Abstract
Recovering an object's 3D structure and camera motion from a few uncalibrated views is a fundamental problem in computer vision. Classical structure from motion methods recover 3D geometry consistent with the input images but cannot recover unseen regions; native generation methods produce plausible 3D objects which are related but not necessarily faithful to the input images. Methods simply concatenating reconstruction and generation allow error to cascade across the two stages, instead of producing a 3D object consistent with both the distribution and the input. We propose GenSfM, which integrates image-based 3D reconstruction with a native 3D diffusion model. In particular, we use a multi-modal diffusion transformer to jointly denoise the shape latent and per-view pose latents under shared self-attention, grounding generated geometry in the observed views while using object-level priors to infer unobserved regions. A compact 3D-native UV-volume tokenization keeps the joint sequence small as views are added, and a pose-aware stage refines geometry and texture by sampling per-voxel image features at the recovered camera poses. On Toys4k and GSO, for $4$ uncalibrated views, GenSfM improves novel-view PSNR by $3.2$-$4.5$ dB and reduces median Chamfer distance by $2.8$-$3.8\times$ over a strong native-3D generation baseline, while recovering input-view poses over $96\%$ Acc@$5^\circ$ without any external pose-estimation backbone, post-hoc optimization, or known intrinsics. Does VGGT Know What It Does Not Know?CORE7
Authors: Aditya Srinivasan, David Nordström
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs
Abstract
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models such as VGGT regress cameras and geometry in a single forward pass, but they report no uncertainty for the camera poses they predict: an incorrect relative pose looks no different from a correct one. We ask whether the frozen model nonetheless knows when its pose is wrong. On HardMatch, a benchmark of 1,000 deliberately difficult image pairs on which VGGT fails on 35% of pairs, a linear probe on VGGT's frozen features predicts pose failure on held-out categories with ROC-AUC 93.5%, compared with 79.7% for VGGT's own point-map confidence and 77.7% for a classifier trained on all of VGGT's outputs. The probe catches failures VGGT is confident about, lets a downstream system abstain on the pairs it distrusts, and transfers without retraining to rotation failures on PhotoTourism and ScanNet-1500, beyond what co-visibility explains. This knowledge is absent from the per-image encoder: it is computed partway through the layers that fuse the two views, and the same holds for a second model, $\pi^3$. In essence, we find that VGGT knows more about its own failures than it reports.

PrinGS: Privacy Preservation in Gaussian SplattingCORE5
Authors: Gaurav Behera, Abhinav Raundhal, Archisha Panda, Saumya Balina, Gargi Shroff...
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has rapidly become the preferred representation for high-fidelity novel view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction. However, the learned representation encodes sensitive visual information including human faces, license plates, documents, and computer screens, raising significant privacy concerns when trained splats are shared or deployed. In this work, we address the underexplored problem of privacy preservation in Gaussian Splatting by proposing a representation-level anonymization framework that directly anonymizes a trained splat. Given a trained splat and a user-provided reference view identifying sensitive content, our method localizes the corresponding regions in the underlying 3D representation and directly anonymizes them, ensuring that the sensitive information is absent from all subsequently rendered novel views while preserving the overall scene geometry and visual fidelity. We achieve this post-training anonymization in less than 1 second. Furthermore, we extend our framework with a privacy-by-design training strategy that prevents sensitive content from being embedded into the representation during optimization, enabling the generation of privacy-preserving splats directly from training images. Our method achieves a remarkably low Face Similarity Measure (FSM) of 9.79% at a retained Face Detection Rate (FDR) of 39.47% and makes sensitive text completely unreadable with an OCR Character Error Rate of 1.00. Our work aims to serve as the primary stepping stone towards privacy-aware 3D representations and motivates further research along these directions.

An Empirical Study on Learning Rooftop Solar Potential from Satellite Imagery Directly Without Explicit GeometryCORE3
Authors: Joshua Lee
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Rooftop solar potential estimation often relies on LiDAR, digital surface models (DSMs), or pipelines that explicitly reconstruct roof geometry before estimating solar potential. We propose Direct Rooftop Solar Prediction (DRSP), an end-to-end formulation that maps satellite imagery and coarse regional sunlight information directly to application-level solar outputs. DRSP neither requires explicit geometric representations as input nor predicts roof orientation, slope, height, DSMs, or other geometric quantities as intermediate outputs, and therefore requires no geometric intermediate supervision. To enable large-scale study of this formulation, we develop a programmatic data-curation pipeline that converts per-panel information from the Google Solar API into usable-rooftop masks and spatial solar-energy targets, yielding two geographically distinct datasets containing approximately 60K rooftops from the U.S. Midwest and 87K from Texas. We demonstrate the feasibility of DRSP and systematically characterize the effects of geographic training distribution, model depth, image resolution, regional sunlight conditioning, and heatmap regression objectives. Overall, our results suggest that useful rooftop-scale solar information can be learned directly from 2D imagery without explicit geometric intermediate representations, offering a simpler path toward large-scale solar assessment. Our code and dataset are available at https://github.com/joshualee124/direct_rooftop_solar_prediction.

How Much Sensing is Enough? Landslide Detection from Sparse and Noisy 3D ObservationsCORE3
Authors: Arijit Pal, Abhijit Mohanty, Dipanjyoti Paul, Sandip Chakraborty, Basabdatta Palit
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs
Abstract
Dense 3D terrain information is critical for accurate landslide detection; however, acquiring such data at large scale is often limited by sensing infrastructure, coverage, and environmental conditions. Emerging sensing modalities (e.g., mmWave, low-cost radar, sparse LiDAR) can operate under adverse conditions, but inherently produce sparse and noisy 3D observations, raising an important question: \emph{how much sensing is sufficient for reliable landslide detection?} Given the absence of large-scale sparse-terrain datasets, we have constructed a physics-informed dataset by combining digital elevation models from OpenTopography with soil moisture data from NASA's SMAP. Subsequently, we have randomly generated landslide events and introduced controlled sparsification and stochastic noise to emulate sensing constraints. We have then developed a learning-based framework to reconstruct the underlying terrain and identify landslide-prone regions using a CNN + XGBoost method. Our results show that with only 20\% of the original observations, the proposed method achieves a detection accuracy of 80\% and a ROC-AUC of 0.868, with a reconstruction error (L2 Chamfer Distance) of 99.67~$m^2$ on the test set. We have further studied the impact of sparsity, highlighting the trade-off between sensing density and detection performance.

Abstract
This article examines the historical experience of the Islamic University of Rotterdam (IUR), today known as the Islamic University of Applied Sciences Rotterdam (IUASR), as a case for understanding cooperation across religious, ethnic, cultural, institutional, and worldview differences beyond conventional interfaith dialogue. Drawing primarily on archival and institutional materials from the university’s founding and first-generation period under its founder and first rector, Dr. S.T.K. Damra, and supplemented by religious, ethical, and historical sources, the study adopts a qualitative historical-conceptual approach. The archival evidence is analysed reconstructively to identify recurring patterns of cooperation, institutional participation, shared responsibility, and societal contribution. The analysis reconstructs the IUR Model of Pluralistic Cooperation around six interrelated principles: orientation towards the common good, preservation of distinct identities, multi-layered cooperation, civic responsibility and shared ownership, inclusive institutional partnerships, and shared social contribution. Particular attention is given to the institutionalization of interreligious engagement through the Werkgroep Interreligieuze Dialoog (Interreligious Dialogue Working Group, WID), its multi-religious composition, and related institutional initiatives. The findings indicate that sustained cooperation may develop where actors identify shared societal objectives, recognize one another as legitimate partners, preserve distinctive identities, assume shared responsibility, and participate through institutional structures capable of sustaining collective action. The article’s principal conceptual contribution lies in positioning interfaith dialogue within a broader framework of pluralistic cooperation that connects recognition and trust with institutional partnership, shared responsibility, and societal contribution. The IUR Model is presented as a context-sensitive analytical reconstruction whose wider applicability requires further comparative examination across different social and institutional settings.

Complexity-guided Regularization for Generalizable Human Gaussian SplattingCORE3
Authors: Gun-Hee Ryu, Gi-Mun Um, Won-Sik Cheong, Wonjun Kim
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs · Code1
Abstract
Recent studies on generalizable human Gaussian splatting have achieved the notable progress in synthesizing novel views of unseen human subjects from multi-view images. Despite this progress, existing methods still struggle to represent fine-grained details of human subjects, which inherently involve high geometric and textural complexity. This is because previous methods do not impose a constraint on how densely Gaussians are placed, which often leads to blurry rendering results in complex regions. To address this problem, we propose a complexity-guided regularization scheme. The key idea is to leverage a complexity prior computed via the local entropy in geometric and textural characteristics of a clothed human. This effectively prevents Gaussians from being coarsely placed in complex regions, which facilitates the faithful reconstruction of intricate structures. Furthermore, we sample local patches predominantly from complex regions according to this complexity prior, which allows the model to be sufficiently supervised by local details. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method delivers state-of-the-art performance while maintaining the real-time inference speed in generalizable human Gaussian splatting. The code and model are publicly available at https://github.com/DCVL-3D/CGR-GS_release.
Summary / 总结

DimFlow: Bidirectional Grid-Expert Learning for Dynamic Gaussian ReconstructionPROXY2
Authors: Gabriel Adineera, Jinoh Kim
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs
Abstract
Dynamic Gaussian reconstruction requires compact scene features to support coordinated changes in geometry and appearance. A factorized grid provides efficient local queries, but accurate interpolation alone does not ensure that the resulting dynamic attributes remain compatible across space and time. We introduce DimFlow, a framework that learns these features through bidirectional interaction between a multi-resolution grid and a coordinate-based expert branch. Feature-level adversarial alignment connects their distributions, adaptive fusion transfers complementary information, and activation-masked residual feedback refines the grid during training. Four attribute experts organize deformation, rotation, opacity, and temporal information before Gaussian rendering. In the aggregate comparison across D-NeRF, HyperNeRF, and DAVIS, DimFlow achieves 39.73 dB PSNR, improving on 4D-GS by 1.92 dB while matching its reported 85 FPS rendering speed. The model occupies 18.9 MB, compared with 18.3 MB for 4D-GS. Component and attribute-expert ablations reveal complementary contributions from feature alignment, adaptive exchange, and temporal processing.

Abstract
Recent research has introduced a novel method for millimeter-wave (mmWave) imaging that leverages surface normal estimation for 3D object reconstruction. Unlike traditional voxel-based or ray-tracing methods, this approach estimates surfaces directly, demonstrating dramatically improved resolution. However, despite its impressive performance for reconstructing relatively smooth surfaces, this new approach suffers from normal-based interference, where closely spaced surfaces with different normals, such as the stitches on a baseball, create interference in the normal estimation process, resulting in inaccurate reconstructions. In this work-in-progress paper, we present a mmWave super-resolution reconstruction method. Our method introduces normal vectors as a new dimension of separability, allowing us to isolate reflections from closely spaced surface components through their different orientations. To do so, since surface components with different normal vectors will reflect mmWave signals in different directions, we separately analyze portions of the aperture to isolate each surface component's reflections. This allows us to accurately estimate normal vectors in high-interference regimes, with our preliminary results showing that this leads to strong reconstruction performance compared to our state-of-the-art baseline, with applications spanning robotics, shipping and logsitics, and augmented reality.

Efficient Multi-modal Video Fusion Network with Dynamic Region-Guided Temporal InteractionECO1

Venue: Information Fusion

Abstract
Multi-modal video fusion aims to generate temporally coherent videos with salient targets and rich structural details by integrating complementary information from heterogeneous sensors. However, existing video fusion methods commonly rely on optical flow estimation and feature warping for inter-frame alignment and cross-frame information aggregation to maintain temporal consistency, thereby introducing considerable computational overhead and making the fusion performance vulnerable to flow estimation errors. To address these limitations, this paper proposes an efficient multi-modal video fusion network with dynamic region-guided temporal interaction. Specifically, the proposed spatio-temporal interaction module leverages the Test-Time Training paradigm to model temporal dependencies with linear complexity. Building on this design, the module restricts temporal modeling to key dynamic regions in the video sequence, as static background regions provide limited temporal cues. By excluding low-variation tokens from unnecessary temporal interaction, this selective strategy contributes to improved fusion quality and temporal consistency. In addition, leveraging the strong ability of pretrained MAE features to capture global structural dependencies, we introduce a feature-level temporal consistency constraint to suppress local flickering by aligning the inter-frame variation directions of the fused and source videos. We further propose a new MAE-based temporal metric that quantifies directional distance between their temporal variations within dynamic regions. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared with existing state-of-the-art methods, our method achieves superior fusion quality and temporal consistency on multiple benchmark datasets, while exhibiting high computational efficiency under edge-device deployment settings.
