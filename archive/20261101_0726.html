Farmland Soil pH in Guizhou, China: Spatial Patterns and Associated FactorsCORE10
Authors: Qin-Yin Pang, Chang-Lin Xu, Rong-Guo Sun, Yu Li
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-10-25
Abs · PDF
Abstract
The spatial structure and multivariable associations of farmland soil pH remain poorly resolved in mountainous landscapes. We analyzed 487 topsoil samples (0–30 cm) from Guizhou, China, using spatial autocorrelation, geostatistics, random forest (RF), and structural equation modeling (SEM). Soil pH ranged from 4.37 to 8.45, with 17.9% of samples below the pH 5.5 screening threshold. Because sampling was not area-weighted, this percentage does not estimate province-wide areal prevalence. Coordinate-level means of pH, soil organic matter (SOM), and cation exchange capacity (CEC) exhibited modest positive global spatial autocorrelation. Local pH evidence after multiplicity correction was limited and neighborhood-dependent. The pH semivariogram indicated short-range dependence and substantial unresolved variation. RF showed modest performance under random and spatially blocked nested cross-validation, with higher errors under spatial validation. CEC ranked first in importance, followed by climate- and terrain-related variables. SEM explained approximately one-fifth of pH variance. Within the model, CEC had the largest standardized direct coefficient in the pH equation, SOM showed a positive indirect association with pH through CEC, and an ordered landform-relief score showed a negative total conditional association. Climate-related pathways yielded small, nonsignificant total associations. These findings inform regional screening and field-level diagnosis of soil acidity, nutrient availability, and exchange status.

Acacia nilotica Biochar Improves Sandy Soil Structure and Stimulates Root Development in Muscat GrapeCORE10
Authors: Ghulam Murtaza, Muhammad Usman, Rashid Iqbal
Venue: Nature &amp; Science
Published: 2026-10-25
Abs · PDF
Abstract
The effectiveness of biochar as a soil amendment is well recognized for improving soil fertility and plant root architecture. However, its beneficial effects on root development in perennial woody species require further investigation. In this 14-week greenhouse experiment, we evaluated the effects of Acacia nilotica-derived biochar on soil physical properties and Muscat grape growth. Muscat grape plants were grown in sandy soil amended with four biochar rates, 4%, 8%, 12%, and 16%, based on dry soil weight. Soil properties and root traits were then assessed. The results showed that adding 16% biochar to sandy soil reduced soil bulk density by 41%, increased total porosity by 51%, and improved water holding capacity by 1.11 times compared with sandy soil without biochar. Biochar incorporation also reduced soil acidity and improved soil buffering capacity, resulting in a more favorable soil pH several months after application. In addition, biochar stimulated fine root development, significantly increasing root length, root crossings, and root forks while reducing average root diameter. Overall, Acacia nilotica wood waste can be converted into valuable biochar for improving sandy soil quality and promoting grape root development. This approach supports waste recycling, improves soil physical conditions, enhances grape growth, and contributes to circular economy-based agriculture. Therefore, Acacia nilotica-derived biochar can be recommended as a promising soil amendment for Muscat grape cultivation, particularly in sandy soils where improved water retention and root growth are needed.

Abstract
Biochar is a promising soil management strategy to reduce reactive nitrogen (N r ) losses from agricultural soils, and replacing mineral fertilizers with organic amendments (OAs) can improve soil health and enhance nutrient recycling. We conducted a three‐year field experiment with maize ( Zea mays ) in Lower Austria to compare organic waste compost and digestates with mineral nitrogen–phosphorus–potassium (NPK) fertilization (175 kg N ha −1 , 39 kg P ha −1 , 187 kg K ha −1 ) and to evaluate the effects of biochar application on nitrous oxide (N 2 O), ammonia (NH 3 ) emissions, and crop yield. Compost and digestates were applied at higher total N rates (350 kg N ha −1 ). Biochar (approximately 7.5 t ha −1 ) was applied in combination with NPK or compost at the same fertilizer application rates as the respective non‐biochar treatments to assess its potential to mitigate gaseous N r losses. Treatment effects on soil properties and microbial functional gene abundances were also evaluated. Overall, compost reduced cumulative N 2 O and NH 3 emissions by 66% and 68%, respectively, whereas digestate increased emissions by 21% (N 2 O) and 32% (NH 3 ) compared to NPK. Biochar‐amended soils mitigated N 2 O emissions by 48% under NPK and 39% under compost. Moreover, NH 3 volatilization was reduced by 51% for NPK and 26% for compost when biochar was added. In reference to yield, digestates successfully substituted NPK, whereas compost did not provide equivalent short‐term nutrient supply due to limited N‐mineralization rates. Soil organic carbon and total nitrogen (TN) increased under OAs, indicating long‐term fertility benefits. Reduced N 2 O emissions under biochar amendment could be linked to a lower denitrification potential in these treatments, while the availability of nitrate () and ammonium () was not affected by biochar amendment, as confirmed by analysis of abundance of N cycling genes and soil extractions. Our research demonstrates that both OAs and biochar can serve as climate‐smart agriculture tools by improving soil fertility and supporting C sequestration while reducing direct N 2 O and NH 3 emissions. However, effective fertilization management is necessary to maintain high yields, requiring integrated nutrient management strategies that strategically combine OAs with mineral N fertilization.

Venue: Anadolu Orman Araştırmaları Dergisi

Abstract
Background and aims This study compared the properties of 0–20 cm topsoil between elevation-matched north- and south-facing hillslopes in the Limne River Basin. Methods Twenty composite samples were collected from ten matched blocks and analyzed for particle-size distribution, pH, soil organic matter (SOM), electrical conductivity (EC), and calcium carbonate equivalent (CCE). Differences were evaluated using exact paired randomization tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, effect sizes, and Holm correction. Results North-facing sites had less sand (43.87% vs. 54.51%; adjusted p = 0.035) and more silt (32.78% vs. 21.09%; adjusted p = 0.014) and SOM (7.59% vs. 4.48%; adjusted p = 0.039). The clay, pH, EC, and CCE levels did not differ significantly. Seven northern samples were loam and three were clay loam, whereas seven southern samples were sandy clay loam, and the others were sandy loam, loam, and clay loam. Conclusions The hillslopes differed in particle-size distribution, textural-class distribution, and SOM; however, because vegetation, slope gradient, and potentially lithology also varied, the results were not interpreted as the independent causal effect of aspect.
Summary / 总结
This study examined whether topsoil properties differ between elevation-matched north- and south-facing hillslopes in the Limne River Basin, motivated by the need to understand aspect-related soil variability in topsoil. Twenty composite 0–20 cm samples were collected from ten matched blocks and analyzed for particle-size distribution, pH, soil organic matter, electrical conductivity, and calcium carbonate equivalent, with differences assessed using exact paired randomization tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, effect sizes, and Holm correction. North-facing sites had less sand (43.87% vs. 54.51%), more silt (32.78% vs. 21.09%), and higher soil organic matter (7.59% vs. 4.48%), whereas clay, pH, electrical conductivity, and calcium carbonate equivalent did not differ significantly; texture classes also differed, with northern samples mostly loam and clay loam and southern samples mostly sandy clay loam, sandy loam, loam, and clay loam. The authors concluded that the hillslopes differed in particle-size distribution, textural-class distribution, and soil organic matter but did not interpret these differences as the independent causal effect of aspect because vegetation, slope gradient, and potentially lithology also varied.

Abstract
Rhizosphere bacteria combining biocontrol activity with resilience to environmental stress are valuable candidates for sustainable agriculture. This study characterized strain DLB3, isolated from black pepper rhizosphere soil, through dual-culture and disk-diffusion antagonism assays, enzymatic and siderophore screening, physiological tolerance tests, antibiotic susceptibility testing, whole-genome sequencing, and genome mining. Phylogenomic analysis and average nucleotide identity (>97%) and digital DNA–DNA hybridization (>79%) values identified the isolate as Bacillus velezensis. The strain inhibited Fusarium falciforme, Fusarium oxysporum, and Colletotrichum gloeosporioides (47.6–71.2% radial growth inhibition) and produced an inhibition zone (8.4 mm) against Ralstonia solanacearum, alongside extracellular protease, amylase, CMCase, and catecholate-type siderophore production. DLB3 tolerated a broad range of temperatures (20–50 °C, with optimal growth at 25–40 °C, peaking at 35 °C), pH (5–9), and salinity (up to 10% NaCl), and exhibited minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of 1800, 500, 450, and 250 mg L−1 for Pb, Zn, Cu, and Cr, respectively, while remaining susceptible to all nine antibiotics tested. The 3,913,361 bp draft genome was assembled into 44 contigs (N50 = 990,239 bp; L50 = 2), with 99.81% completeness and 0.12% contamination according to CheckM. It harbored 18 biosynthetic gene clusters, carbohydrate-active enzymes, peptidases, and genes associated with heavy metal resistance, broadly consistent with the observed phenotypes, as well as a homolog of the cfr methyltransferase gene (97.4% identity) that was not phenotypically expressed. These findings provide a comprehensive genomic and phenotypic profile of strain DLB3 and its biocontrol-related potential.

Abstract
Water scarcity and the need for more efficient nitrogen management are major constraints to sustainable dryland agriculture. Fertilization strategies that improve crop productivity while increasing water and nitrogen use efficiency (WUE and NUE) are therefore important for intercropping systems. We conducted a 2‐year field experiment to evaluate the effects of slow‐release fertilizer mixed with urea on yield, WUE, NUE, and economic returns in a corn ( Zea mays L.)–sunflower ( Helianthus annuus L.) intercropping system. Three fertilization treatments were established: slow‐release fertilizer (S), urea (U), and slow‐release fertilizer mixed with urea at a 7:3 ratio (M), combined with two row configurations: 2 rows of corn ‖ 2 rows of sunflower (C 2 S 2 ) and 2 rows of corn ‖ 4 rows of sunflower (C 2 S 4 ). Compared with single‐fertilizer treatments, the M treatments generally produced greater leaf area index and aboveground dry matter accumulation in both crops and higher soil organic matter and total nitrogen in the 0–20 cm soil layer. Compared with urea alone, MC 2 S 2 increased total system yield by up to 36.7%, WUE by up to 26.70% (and by up to 37.02% compared with UC 2 S 4 in 2022), and NUE by up to 28.6%. The MC 2 S 2 treatment also produced the highest system yield and net income, with a 2‐year average of 37,650 yuan ha −1 , exceeding the other treatments by 4.13%–41.57% in 2021. Overall, the combination of slow‐release fertilizer and urea at a 7:3 ratio with C 2 S 2 configuration was the best performing treatment combination among those evaluated under the experimental conditions. These findings support further evaluation of mixed slow‐release fertilizer and urea strategies across locations, years, fertilizer ratios, and nitrogen rates in corn–sunflower intercropping systems.

Abstract
Irrigated Calcisols in the Fergana Valley (Uzbekistan) have recently been intensively used for the cultivation of vineyards with various varieties of grapes. The biogeochemistry of a number of chemical elements and their translocation from soil to plants was investigated. The behavior of trace elements was evaluated by instrumental neutron activation analysis. The soil content of Mn, Mo, Zn and Cu was regulated by the duration and intensity of irrigation. The maximum concentrations of microelements were in leaves and the minimum were in the fruits of grapes. Integral indicators of the accumulation of trace elements in the “soil–plant” system revealed a limiting of the flow of trace elements into the generative organs and low bioavailability of trace elements for the crop. Irrigated light Calcisols in the study area have a different duration of prescription for irrigation; arable horizons of recently irrigated soils have a lesser humus horizon than short irrigated soils and long irrigated soils. The duration and intensity of irrigation have a decisive effect on the morphology and biogeochemistry of Calcisols and on the behavior of biomicroelements in the soil–grape system. Viticulture in the Fergana Valley is experiencing water scarcity risks; thus, continued monitoring of how soil and vineyards respond to water availability levels is needed. The grapes grown on irrigated soils have effective physiological mechanisms that limit the accumulation of trace elements in the food part.
Summary / 总结
This study examined how irrigation and land cultivation affect biomicroelement contents in grape organs and soil quality in irrigated Calcisols of the Fergana Valley, Uzbekistan, where vineyards are intensively used and water scarcity is a risk. Trace-element behavior was assessed by instrumental neutron activation analysis in soils with different irrigation durations and intensities and in grape organs. The results showed that soil Mn, Mo, Zn, and Cu contents were regulated by irrigation duration and intensity; microelement concentrations were highest in leaves and lowest in fruits, and integrated soil–plant accumulation indicators indicated limited trace-element flow into generative organs and low bioavailability to the crop. Irrigation duration and intensity decisively influenced Calcisol morphology and biogeochemistry and soil–grape system behavior, recently irrigated soils had a thinner humus horizon than short- and long-irrigated soils, and the grapes showed physiological mechanisms limiting trace-element accumulation in the food part.
